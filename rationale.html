Design rationale

One protocol, six lenses.

Seer is an Information Finance protocol. This design starts from that idea, then uses the Electric Foundation kit as its foundation and rethinks the experience where the kit stops.

The idea behind the structure

Seer is a two-sided marketplace. Information seekers need answers to make decisions. Traders (people and AI agents) put capital behind forecasts for a potential profit. The market price sits between them as a shared, transparent signal.

So every screen is built around three questions a visitor should be able to answer within seconds:

1
Who is asking?Every market card names the asker and their type (DAO, research team, nonprofit, public). The home page shows both sides of the marketplace, with a path for each.
2
What is the market pricing?A plain-language “Prices” line sits on each card, so a number like 69% always says what it is a probability of.
3
How do I use the signal?Each interface carries a “How to read this signal” strip, and each market detail view ends with a usage note: evidence, not a verdict.

Seer App as the Etherscan of Seer

The app is a general explorer, not a trading screen. People can search by question, asker or market ID, filter by market type and category, and open any market for its price history, recent trades (humans and agents labelled), resolution details and asker. From there they can trade, or jump to the interface built for that market type.

What stays from EF

Logo and wordmark, the electric violet (#7435F8), the dark and light palettes, nested-surface cards, Yes/No pill buttons, the Up/Down and three-way buttons, the Amount + Confirm ticket, and the market-card footer (volume · time · category).

What changed from the kit, and why

1
Semantic status coloursAdded green / amber / red for gains, risk levels and agent P&L. The kit has none; risk and conditional forecasts need a fast read.
2
Yes/No prices sum to 100¢The kit mock shows 69¢ / 37¢. The ticket uses complementary pricing (69¢ / 31¢) so numbers stay honest.
3
Mono labels for dataJetBrains Mono for eyebrows, axes and metrics, to separate “data” from “narrative” in dense screens.
4
Soft violet glow in heroesOne ambient gradient per page ties the six sites together and signals the protocol layer.
5
Table views for Opportunity & RiskComparison tasks need aligned columns, not cards.
6
Persistent “informs, doesn’t decide” notesFutarchy, Opportunity and Risk all repeat that markets are one input, never the verdict.
7
An asker line on every market cardThe kit card shows the question and prices only. Adding who asked turns an anonymous number into context, which is what a two-sided marketplace needs.
8
“Prices” and “How to use it” in plain wordsNew text blocks, in the same nested surface as the kit, explain the signal without extra chrome.
9
A market detail view with explorer-style dataMarket ID, volume, liquidity, trades and resolution make Seer App feel like an explorer for the whole protocol.
10
Home page built around the marketplaceThe hero and a seekers-and-traders section replace a generic “forecasting layer” story.

Interface intent

Home shows Seer as the protocol beneath its apps. Seer App is the Etherscan-style explorer for every market. Deep maps many-outcome questions and agent activity. Futarchy compares pass vs fail forecasts. Opportunity shows selection odds while reviewers keep the call. Risk Pricing compares protocols side by side.

Accessibility

Both themes meet contrast for body text, interactive elements are keyboard-reachable, motion respects prefers-reduced-motion, and every layout collapses cleanly to phone width.
